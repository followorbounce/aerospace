# TILT — Uranus & Neptune, the ice giants (Wave 5) — author notes

- **Slug / category:** `tilt-uranus-neptune`, `future` (wiring not done by the author team).
- **Purpose:** explain the ice giants through what makes them hard: Uranus's 97.77° tilt, extreme distance (sunlight,
  power, radio), the single Voyager 2 visit to each, the long trip out, and the Uranus Orbiter and Probe's real status.
- **Audience:** curious general readers and students, same as the other field guides. Assumes high-school physics only.
- **Owner:** aerospace field-guide maintainer. Sources in this folder; the committed page is generated by `_tools/assemble.py`.
- **Structure:** 8 numbered chapters (lede says "Eight chapters", REFERENCES included, matching every other generated guide:
  SAIL 6/6 "Six chapters", POLAR 8/8 "Eight chapters"). Note: the older memory note says REFERENCES isn't counted; the
  generated guides all count it, so this follows them.
- **Instruments:** (1) seasons: daily-mean insolation vs latitude through one orbit, Uranus/Neptune/Earth; (2) far and dim:
  sunlight, panel area for 350 W, light time, plus Voyager data-rate chart (DESCANSO Table 6-1); (3) encounter geometry to
  scale (Uranus/Neptune toggle); (4) two-body transfer explorer (v∞ → C3, flight time, arrival v∞).

## Assumptions and limitations
- Seasons: circular orbit, even pace; real eccentricity (0.047) changes Uranus's sunlight by about ±10 % and shifts
  season timing. Top-of-atmosphere daily means, not temperatures. Latitude is in the hemisphere lit first after equinox.
- Transfer: Sun-only two-body, circular coplanar planet orbits, tangential departure; no gravity assists, no capture
  burn modelled. The flight time is extremely sensitive near the Hohmann minimum, so the slider snaps to the exact
  Hohmann speed when within 0.006 km/s.
- Panel area: 30 % cell efficiency is an assumption, not a mission value. Light time uses Sun distance (±8 min).
- "1979–81 ground system" bars are DESCANSO's expected values, not my 1/r² estimate (which agrees within 10 %).
- Encounter figure: only planet radius, closest-approach altitude and moon orbit are to scale; the path is schematic.
