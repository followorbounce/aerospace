CH.01 / 09 — MOMENTUM

A rocket pushes against its own exhaust

A rocket brings its own thing to push against: propellant.

Thrust is mass flow times exhaust velocity.

CH.02 / 09 — THE ROCKET EQUATION AND STAGING

Speed grows only with the logarithm of mass

Δv equals exhaust velocity times the logarithm of the mass ratio.

Staging: the Δv of the stages add up.

TSIOLKOVSKY'S ROCKET EQUATION; ONE STAGE OF A STAGED ROCKET
Δv = ve ln(m0/m1)  ·  Δvtotal = Σ ve,i ln Ri  ·  πi = (1/Ri − ε)/(1 − ε)
The best split for identical stages is equal Δv each.

CH.03 / 09 — ORBITS AND VIS-VIVA

An orbit is a fall that misses

Circular speed is √(GM/r); escape speed is √2 times that.

Vis-viva gives the speed anywhere on any orbit.

VIS-VIVA, CIRCULAR AND ESCAPE SPEED, KEPLER'S THIRD LAW
v² = GM (2/r − 1/a)  ·  vcirc = √(GM/r)  ·  vesc = √(2GM/r)  ·  T = 2π√(a³/GM)
r is measured from the centre of the body.

CH.04 / 09 — HOHMANN TRANSFERS AND PLANE CHANGES

Two burns move you between circles; turning the plane costs more

Hohmann's two-burn transfer.

A plane change costs 2v sin(Δi/2).

HOHMANN TRANSFER WITH THE PLANE CHANGE FOLDED INTO THE OUTER BURN
a = (r1 + r2)/2  ·  Δv1 = |v(r1, a) − vcirc(r1)|  ·  Δv2 = √(vt² + vc² − 2 vt vc cos Δi)  ·  t = π√(a³/GM)
Burns are treated as instantaneous.

CH.05 / 09 — THE OBERTH EFFECT

Burn where you are fastest

The same Δv adds more energy to a faster craft.

One burn in low orbit: 3.61 km/s instead of 6.17.

vp = √(v∞² + 2GM/rp)  ·  v∞′ = √((vp + Δv)² − 2GM/rp)  ·  ΔE/m = vpΔv + Δv²/2

CH.07 / 09 — WAYS TO MAKE THE PUSH

High thrust or high exhaust velocity, rarely both

Chemical engines top out near 4.5 km/s of exhaust velocity.

At fixed power, doubling the exhaust velocity halves the thrust.

POWER IN THE JET
Pjet = ½ ṁ ve² = ½ F ve  ·  F = 2ηP / ve
Jet power is half thrust times exhaust velocity.
