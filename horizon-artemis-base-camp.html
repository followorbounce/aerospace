CH.01 / 10 — WHY THE SOUTH POLE

Ice in the shadows, light on the ridges

Apollo landed near the equator, where sunlight was reliable and terrain was flat. Artemis is aimed at the opposite kind of ground: the region around the lunar south pole, where craters that never see sunlight are cold enough to trap water ice for billions of years, beside ridges that catch long stretches of light — resource and power in the same neighbourhood. [1]

Water ice means drinking water, breathable oxygen, and — split into hydrogen and oxygen — rocket propellant, without hauling it all from Earth. That's the resource a permanent base is built around.

CH.04 / 10 — FROM GATEWAY TO SURFACE-FIRST

A space station, paused mid-build

Gateway was designed as a small lunar-orbit station — a staging point where astronauts would transfer between Orion and a landing craft, with contributions from NASA, ESA, JAXA, the CSA and the UAE. On 24 March 2026 NASA said it intends to pause Gateway in its current form and shift to infrastructure for sustained surface operations, repurposing applicable equipment. [6] In April 2026 NASA stopped work on the HALO habitation module, whose contract had grown to $1.9 billion while corrosion was being repaired; [7] in August NASA said HALO power and avionics hardware is being repurposed for technology payloads bound for the lunar surface. CH.08 / 10 — SURVIVING THE LUNAR NIGHT

Fourteen Earth days without the Sun

Over most of the Moon a day-night cycle lasts about 29.5 Earth days — roughly two weeks of sunlight followed by two weeks of cold darkness. [11] Even the best-lit polar ridges spend some of the year in shadow, so solar power alone can’t carry a permanent base. Step through the plan.

CH.09 / 10 — RISKS AND UNCERTAINTY

A plan is not a guarantee

Every major spaceflight program slips its early schedules, and a base camp with this many new, untested pieces has more than the usual number of ways to slip.

CH.10 / 11 — LEGACY

A rehearsal for Mars, if it works

Every system a lunar base has to solve — life support that runs for years unattended, power through two-week nights, resource extraction from local ice — is also, deliberately, a rehearsal for a much longer, much less forgiving trip to Mars.
