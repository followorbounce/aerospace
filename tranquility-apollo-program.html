CH.01 / 10 — KENNEDY'S DEADLINE

Eight years, four months, and change

On May 25, 1961 — six weeks after Gagarin's flight and just twenty days after Alan Shepard's fifteen minutes above the atmosphere — President Kennedy committed the United States to landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely, before the decade was out. At the time, America had a grand total of fifteen minutes of crewed spaceflight experience.

Apollo 11 touched down on July 20, 1969 — with five months to spare.

CH.07 / 10 — APOLLO 13

Failure is not an option

On April 13, 1970, an oxygen tank in the service module exploded, crippling the spacecraft's power, water, and life support two days from the Moon. The lunar landing was abandoned within hours; the entire mission became a single objective — getting three men home alive.

The crew used the still-intact lunar module as a lifeboat, rationing power and water while ground teams improvised a carbon-dioxide filter fix from spare parts and duct tape. All three astronauts splashed down safely on April 17 — a failure that became, by NASA's own account, its finest hour.
