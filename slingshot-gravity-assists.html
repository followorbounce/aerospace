CH.01 / 09 — THE PROBLEM

The outer planets are far, and the cheap way there is slow

The cheapest route is a Hohmann transfer.

Voyager 2 reached Neptune in twelve years.

CH.02 / 09 — THE TURN

The planet only changes the direction — the Sun sees a new speed

Relative to the planet, speed in equals speed out; only the direction turns.

Cassini's 1999 Earth flyby: computed 5.47 km/s, announced about 5.5.

Not free energy: the planet pays

CH.04 / 09 — FASTER OR SLOWER

Behind the planet to speed up, in front of it to brake

MESSENGER needed six flybys to brake into Mercury orbit.

Galileo's Io pass saved about 90 kg of propellant.

Which side decides faster or slower; how close decides how much.

CH.05 / 09 — OUT OF THE SUN'S GRIP

One pass of Jupiter turns a closed orbit into an escape

One Jupiter flyby can push a probe past solar escape speed.

New Horizons gained about 14,000 km/h at Jupiter.

CH.06 / 09 — WHO THOUGHT OF IT

From a lunar loop to a planetary relay

Luna 3 swung past the Moon in 1959; Minovitch championed the technique in the early 1960s.

Flandro found the 175-year alignment in 1965; Mariner 10 flew the first gravity assist in 1974.

CH.07 / 09 — THE GRAND TOUR

Four planets, twelve years, one spacecraft

NASA cancelled the Grand Tour in 1972; Voyager replaced it.

Voyager 2 visited all four giants.

Direct times are Hohmann transfers.

CH.08 / 09 — LEGACY

Half a century of borrowed speed

Every spacecraft heading out of the Sun's grip took some speed from Jupiter.
