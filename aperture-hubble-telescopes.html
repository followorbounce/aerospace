CH.01 / 10 — SEEING WITHOUT ATMOSPHERE

A 100-km blanket of blur

Earth's atmosphere is wonderful to breathe and terrible to look through. Turbulent air blurs starlight — the same effect that makes stars twinkle — and the atmosphere blocks most ultraviolet and infrared light outright before it ever reaches a ground-based mirror.

More than visible light

Hubble sees ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light — a wider slice of the spectrum than the human eye, but only part of what's out there. Different observatories are built for different bands.
