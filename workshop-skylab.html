CH.01 / 10 — A STATION FROM SPARE PARTS

Built from a Moon rocket's third stage

With Apollo winding down and Saturn V rockets already built and paid for, NASA's Apollo Applications Program looked for a second use for the hardware. Skylab's answer: hollow out the S-IVB third stage — the same stage that sent Apollo crews toward the Moon — and outfit it as a two-storey orbital workshop on the ground before it ever launched.

CH.02 / 10 — LAUNCH DAY DISASTER

Wrecked sixty-three seconds in

On May 14, 1973, barely a minute after launch, aerodynamic forces tore away Skylab's micrometeoroid shield — a lightweight sunshade meant to protect the station from both space debris and overheating. The debris took one solar array completely off the station and jammed the other, leaving Skylab critically short of power and dangerously overheating before a single astronaut had reached it.

Ground controllers scrambled to find a survivable attitude — angling the station to balance power generation against heat — while engineers on the ground had eleven days to invent a fix nobody had planned for.
