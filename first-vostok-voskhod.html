VOSTOK AND VOSKHOD

Between 1961 and 1965 the Soviet Union flew eleven people into orbit.

CH.01 / 11 — A BALL, A CONE AND A SEAT

The simplest shape that comes home the right way up

Vostok was a spherical descent module and a conical instrument module.

The cosmonaut ejected at about 7 km; the low orbit was the backup for the braking engine.

CH.02 / 11 — ONE ORBIT, MINUTE BY MINUTE

“Poyekhali!” — and 108 minutes later, a field near Saratov

Gagarin lifted off at 06:07 UTC into a 181 × 327 km orbit.

Then the instrument module failed to separate.

CH.03 / 11 — BALLISTIC RE-ENTRY

The peak load depends on one angle, not on the spacecraft

Allen and Eggers solved ballistic entry in closed form.

The vehicle changes where the peak happens, not how big it is.

V(h) = VE exp[ −ρ(h) H / (2β sin γ) ]  ·  amax = VE² sin γ / (2eH)  ·  ρ(hmax) = β sin γ / H
ρ(h) = ρ₀ e^(−h/H).

Ten days of air, and an orbit that would have taken twenty

Vostok relied on orbital decay instead of a second braking engine.

CH.05 / 11 — A DAY IN ORBIT, THEN TWO SHIPS AT ONCE

Sleeping, feeling sick, and seeing another spacecraft

Titov flew 17 orbits and was the first to sleep in space.

Vostok 3 and 4 came within about 6.5 km without any engine for changing orbit.

CH.06 / 11 — VOSTOK 6

48 orbits, and nineteen years until the next woman

Tereshkova flew 48 orbits in nearly three days.

No other woman flew until 1982.

CH.07 / 11 — THREE MEN IN A BALL BUILT FOR ONE

Voskhod 1: no spacesuits, no ejection seats, no margin

The Vostok sphere was refitted for three, with no room for spacesuits.

16 orbits with no escape system at all.

CH.08 / 11 — LEONOV OUTSIDE

Twelve minutes outside, and a suit too stiff to come back in

Leonov went outside through an inflatable airlock.

The Berkut suit ballooned; Leonov bled it to the lower setting.
