A FIELD GUIDE TO EUROPA CLIPPER

Nine chapters on the largest spacecraft NASA has ever built for planetary science, sent not to land but to circle a radiation-blasted moon of Jupiter forty-nine times at close range, probing the ice with radar and reading the ocean's presence in its own magnetic field — to find out whether one of the solar system's best candidates for life is, in fact, habitable. CH.01 / 10 — MORE WATER THAN ALL OF EARTH'S OCEANS COMBINED

A moon kept warm inside by the planet squeezing it

Beneath Europa's cracked, icy crust, scientists are confident there is a global ocean of liquid water — plausibly holding more water than all of Earth's oceans combined.[1] It stays liquid not because of sunlight, which is faint that far out, but because Jupiter's gravity flexes the moon as it orbits, generating heat through tidal friction the same way repeatedly bending a paperclip warms it.

CH.02 / 10 — TOO MUCH RADIATION TO STAY CLOSE FOR LONG

Dip in, gather data, and get back out before the radiation adds up

Jupiter's magnetosphere traps intense radiation, and a spacecraft parked in orbit around Europa itself would be cooked within months. Instead, Europa Clipper orbits Jupiter on a long, looping path and dives close to Europa — as near as 25 km — around 49 times over the mission, retreating to a safer distance between each pass to let its electronics recover.[1]

CH.03 / 10 — LAUNCH: THE BIGGEST NASA PLANETARY SPACECRAFT EVER BUILT

Solar arrays the length of a basketball court, because Jupiter is so dim

Europa Clipper launched on a Falcon Heavy on October 14, 2024. With its solar arrays deployed it spans roughly 30 metres, the largest spacecraft NASA has ever built for planetary exploration — sized that large simply because sunlight at Jupiter is about 4% as strong as at Earth, and every bit of collecting area matters.[1]

CH.04 / 10 — BORROWING SPEED FROM TWO PLANETS ON THE WAY

Gravity assists at Mars and Earth, then years of coasting

No rocket can throw a spacecraft this large directly to Jupiter, so Europa Clipper's trajectory borrows momentum along the way: a flyby of Mars for a gravity assist, followed later by one of Earth, before a multi-year cruise out to Jupiter arrival expected around 2030.[1]

CH.06 / 10 — SEEING THROUGH KILOMETRES OF FROZEN CRUST

Looking for water that never made it all the way down to the ocean

The REASON radar instrument sends radio waves into the ice and listens for echoes bounced back by layers and pockets of different density — mapping the shell's thickness and searching for shallow lakes of meltwater that may sit within the ice itself, potentially far easier to sample than the deep ocean below.[1]

CH.07 / 10 — PROOF WITHOUT EVER TOUCHING THE WATER

A salty ocean answers Jupiter's magnetic field like an echo

As Europa moves through Jupiter's powerful, tilted magnetic field, a salty conductive ocean beneath the ice generates its own weak induced field in response — first hinted at by the Galileo mission in the 1990s. Europa Clipper's magnetometer will measure that response in far greater detail, constraining the ocean's depth and salinity without a single instrument touching it.[2]

CH.08 / 10 — IF THE OCEAN EVER VENTS INTO SPACE

Hubble has seen possible plumes; Clipper would fly through one

Telescopes, including Hubble, have reported tentative signs of water-vapour plumes venting from Europa's surface, similar to the confirmed geysers on Saturn's moon Enceladus. If Europa Clipper's trajectory happens to carry it through one, its MASPEX mass spectrometer could sample material fresh from the ocean directly, without drilling through a single metre of ice.[2]

CH.09 / 10 — NOT A LIFE-DETECTION MISSION — A HABITABILITY MISSION

Answering whether the conditions are right, before anyone looks for life itself

Europa Clipper carries no instrument designed to detect life directly. Its job is to determine whether Europa actually has the three ingredients life as we know it needs — liquid water, the right chemistry, and a stable energy source — turning decades of inference from Voyager and Galileo into a detailed, close-up case for or against a future lander mission.[1]

CH.10 / 10 — LEGACY

From a blurry hint to a dedicated mission

Every mission to reach Jupiter has added a piece to the Europa picture without ever being built to study it specifically. Europa Clipper is the first spacecraft designed around this one moon as the entire point of the trip.
