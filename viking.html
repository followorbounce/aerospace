CH.01 / 10 — ESCAPING TO MARS

A window that opens every 26 months

Earth and Mars only line up for an efficient transfer once every 26 months. Launch outside that window and the fuel cost climbs fast; launch inside it, and a spacecraft can coast along a long, curving ellipse — a Hohmann transfer — from Earth's orbit out to meet Mars roughly seven months later.

Adjust the departure burn and see the same spacecraft either fall short, meet Mars, or overshoot into a wider orbit.

CH.02 / 10 — THE VIKING PROGRAM

Two identical spacecraft, two questions

Each Viking spacecraft paired an orbiter, which mapped the planet from above, with a lander that carried its own biology lab to the surface. Designed for 90 days, both missions ran for years. Scrub through the program.

CH.04 / 10 — ANATOMY OF A LANDER

A chemistry lab on three legs

Each Viking lander carried a robot arm, a miniature biology and chemistry lab, a weather station, and a seismometer — all built to run for three months with no possibility of repair. Select a component.

CH.05 / 10 — LANDING WITHOUT A REHEARSAL

Thin air, no second chance

Mars' atmosphere is thick enough to superheat a spacecraft on entry but far too thin to slow one down on parachute alone — a problem every Mars lander since Viking has had to solve in its own way. Step through Viking's sequence.

CH.06 / 10 — WHAT VIKING FOUND

A planet, not just a target

Whatever the biology experiments did or didn't prove, Viking returned the first real, sustained picture of Mars as a place.
